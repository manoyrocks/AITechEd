## 1. Why now
- **Reskilling demand is huge.** WEF 2025 expects 39% of core skills to change by 2030, 59 of every 100 workers to need training, and AI and big data to be the fastest-growing skill. The EU AI Act Art. 4 AI-literacy duty has applied since Feb 2025.
- **"AI 101" is commoditized.** Microsoft Elevate, Google AI Essentials and the labs' free study modes give it away. Being *hands-on and role-specific* is not commoditized.
- **Adults are leaving manipulative learning apps.** The Duolingo backlash came from three things: the AI-first memo ("AI slop"), the Energy system ("punished for using the app?") and streak burnout. People who want serious learning move on to Anki, italki and Pimsleur. **Trust and humans in the loop win.**
- **60+ is clearly under-served.**
  - No top app targets older adults, even though Babbel, Elevate and Impulse draw heavily on them.
  - Timed games and small type dominate.
  - Older adults are the fastest-growing new AI users and also the group most worried about scams.
  - GetSetUp shows that peer-led, live, no-download learning works.
- **Investors favour it.** They prefer "AI-native, career-aligned, outcome-backed" companies. The studio's market research names this the fastest route to $1M ARR (9–18 months).

### App cards

#### 1. AI Fluency Lab (20–59)
- **Problem:** Workers finish AI courses but don't change how they work. Employers can't measure AI skills.
- **Experience:** Pick a role track, then practise inside sandboxed versions of real workflows (drafting a policy, analyzing a spreadsheet, triaging customer emails). Graded projects. Responsible-AI checkpoints (privacy, bias, verification). A "Skills Passport".
- **AI role:** Multiple frontier models in sandboxes. An AI grader with rubrics plus human review for credentials. Adaptive practice.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Screen-reader-friendly sandboxes, keyboard-first, captions, plain-language mode, no timed assessments.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Mid-market employers (500–5,000 staff) will pay $150–400 per seat for role-specific AI fluency over free Big Tech programs.
- **Validation test:** A concierge cohort for 2 design-partner employers (20 staff each), with pre/post task-performance measures, plus 3 paid letters of intent.

#### 2. Career Sprint (20–34)
- **Problem:** Entry-level jobs are shrinking and certificates carry little weight.
- **Experience:** Sprints where learners build real portfolio projects with AI tools. Mock interviews with an AI interviewer and rubric feedback. Hiring-partner demo days.
- **AI role:** Interviewer simulations (clearly labeled), project feedback.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Accommodations in interview practice (extra time, text mode). Captions.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Hiring partners value sprint portfolios.
- **Validation test:** A concierge sprint with 15 learners and 5 hiring managers reviewing portfolios.

#### 3. Speak Freely (20+)
- **Problem:** Learners plateau at A2–B1. Streak culture breeds anxiety. AI-only content is mistrusted.
- **Experience:** Daily scenario conversations (ordering food, meeting in-laws). A weekly 15-minute human tutor check-in. A progress map with **gentle streaks** (pause days, weekly goals).
- **AI role:** A conversation partner that adapts to CEFR level and gives pronunciation feedback. Humans review the curriculum.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Text-chat alternative for people with speech disabilities or stuttering. Captions. Adjustable speaking speed. Large-type preset for 60+.
- **Riskiest assumption:** The hybrid AI + human model has viable unit economics at about $20–30/mo.
- **Validation test:** A price test (landing page) plus a 4-week concierge (n=20) with Wizard-of-Oz AI and real tutors.

#### 4. Lead with AI (35–59)
- **Problem:** Managers must lead AI change without training or a playbook.
- **Experience:** Branching simulations (rollout resistance, an ethics incident, reskilling decisions), peer cohorts and playbook templates.
- **AI role:** Simulation characters (clearly role-play) and debrief coaching.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Text or voice mode, and captions.
- **Riskiest assumption:** L&D buyers will fund manager simulations separately from AI Fluency Lab.
- **Validation test:** Bundle vs. stand-alone pricing interviews with 12 L&D leaders.

#### 5. Parent Coach (25–59)
- **Problem:** Parents want to help with learning and AI use but don't know how. Content is scattered.
- **Experience:** 5-minute lessons ("How to help without giving the answer", "Talking about AI companions"). Scripts. The child's venture dashboard is linked.
- **AI role:** Answers questions from a vetted knowledge base. Personalizes tips to the child's age and needs.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Audio-first for busy parents. Plain language. Multilingual.
- **Riskiest assumption:** This works as a retention and cross-sell engine across ventures rather than a stand-alone revenue line.
- **Validation test:** Measure the effect on child-app retention in the Lanternling and Questwise pilots.

#### 6. Silver Circuit (60+)
- **Problem:** Older adults fear scams and feel left behind by AI. Adult children do the tech support.
- **Experience:**
  - Voice-guided lessons: video-calling grandkids, using AI safely for health questions, spotting AI scams.
  - Weekly live classes led by **peer guides** (older adults).
  - A family member can set things up remotely, **with the senior's consent**.
- **AI role:** A patient voice helper that repeats without judgement. Scam-simulation practice. Explanations at the learner's pace.
- **Accessibility & sensory:**
  - ≥18 px body text, targets of 56 dp or more, high contrast.
  - Linear flows, no timeouts.
  - Captions, hearing-aid-friendly audio.
  - Passkey or family-assisted sign-in (WCAG 3.3.8).
- **Riskiest assumption:** Medicare Advantage plans or libraries will pay, or adult children will buy it as a gift.
- **Validation test:**
  - Library pilot (2 branches).
  - Gift-purchase smoke test aimed at adult children.
  - 5 MA plan / aging-agency discovery calls.

#### 7. Curiosity Circle (55+)
- **Problem:** Isolation and loss of cognitive engagement. Brain games don't transfer, and their timers exclude many older users.
- **Experience:**
  - Cohorts of 8–12 people learning together: Italian for travel, guitar again, local history, family genealogy.
  - Captioned live sessions and an AI study buddy for practice between them.
  - No timed games.
- **AI role:** Practice partner, recap generator, genealogy research helper. Humans lead the cohorts.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Large type, captions, audio descriptions, and a slow pace.
- **Claims discipline:** Social and cognitive *engagement* only. **No** dementia or medical claims (FTC Lumosity precedent).
- **Riskiest assumption:** Enough cohorts fill up, and people stay beyond the first cohort.
- **Validation test:** Run 3 pilot cohorts via libraries and senior centers. Measure attendance and re-enrollment.

## 5. Product principles (Evergrow-specific)
1. **Do, don't watch.** Every skill is practised on a realistic task.
2. **Humans in the loop.** Human tutors, guides and reviewers are part of the value and the brand, never cut to save costs.
3. **No streak guilt.** Gentle progress, pause days and adult respect.
4. **Every stage of life.** The 60+ experience is designed first, not bolted on.
5. **Verified, not vanity.** Skills verification and outcome data over completion badges.

## 6. Business model and go-to-market
- **B2B (lead):**
  - Mid-market employers, via direct sales and LMS/LXP partners (Workday, Cornerstone, Degreed).
  - Workforce boards (WIOA) and community colleges.
  - EU companies meeting their AI Act Art. 4 literacy duty.
- **B2C:** Speak Freely and Career Sprint subscriptions, plus Plus bundles.
- **B2B2C 60+:** Medicare Advantage supplemental benefits, libraries, state aging departments, senior living, AARP-style partners, and family gifting.
- **Moat:**
  - performance-based skills data mapped to roles
  - employer integrations
  - a peer-guide network for 60+
  - a trusted brand that visibly keeps humans in the loop

## 7. Ethics, safety and compliance
- Employee data: consent, no surveillance analytics for managers beyond agreed skills data. GDPR/CCPA.
- 60+: anti-dark-pattern billing, scam-safe communications (we never ask for payment over the phone), and family access only with the senior's consent.
- AI transparency (EU AI Act Art. 50). Human review of credentials. Bias testing of graders.

## 8. Risks and mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Free Big Tech AI literacy | Role-specific, hands-on, verified skills. Mid-market focus |
| Long health-plan sales cycles (60+) | Libraries and family gifting first, then MA plans |
| Unit economics of human tutors | Tiered human time. AI handles practice, humans handle check-ins and cohorts |
| Content goes stale fast as AI tools change | A modular content system. Quarterly refresh with SME review |

## 9. Discovery and validation focus
- **Top assumptions:** employer willingness to pay for role-specific fluency; the 60+ payer (MA, library, family); hybrid human+AI economics for Speak Freely; cohort retention in Curiosity Circle.
- **North-star:** *Verified skills applied per active learner per month.* Guardrails for 60+: confidence score, session comfort, zero billing complaints.
- **Three-year ambition:**
  - Year 1: AI Fluency Lab with 5 paying employers, plus Silver Circuit library pilots.
  - Year 2: Career Sprint, Speak Freely, Lead with AI.
  - Year 3: MA plan contract, and a published skills-outcome study.
