## 1. Why now
- **Clear under-service at ages 1–3.** Almost nothing in the top 30 is built for toddlers, and most toddler screen time is YouTube ([Research paper §4.4](../01-research-paper.md)).
- **Screen-time guilt is the main emotion parents bring.** The AAP's January 2026 policy dropped fixed limits in favour of design quality and co-engagement. "Built for co-play" and "stops itself" are now credible, differentiating claims.
- **Calm tech works.** Pok Pok (2021 Apple Design Award) and Sago Mini (2026 ADA Inclusivity finalist) show parents pay for calm design. The mass-market leaders (ABCmouse, Kiddopia, Lingokids) are high-stimulation and get complaints about paywalls and cancellation (the ABCmouse FTC $10M settlement).
- **Real-time voice AI makes tutoring pre-readers possible.** Frontier labs do not serve under-13s, which leaves the space open.
- **The evidence favours structured literacy and Montessori-style self-correcting play.** The 2025 PNAS Montessori RCT is positive. Structured phonics is well supported.

### App cards

#### 1. Babble Buddy (1–3)
- **Problem:** Toddlers learn language best from responsive adult conversation ("serve and return"). Screens handed to toddlers often replace talk.
- **Experience:** The phone stays with the **parent**. Routine-based prompt cards ("At bath time: name 3 body parts, pause, wait for Leo's reply"), songs and rhymes, and weekly word-growth tracking done by parent tap.
- **AI role:** Suggests prompts tailored to the child's age, interests and new words. Optional **on-device** conversation-turn counting: no audio leaves the phone, and it is off by default.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Voice-guided for parents who read little. Large text. Works one-handed. Uses no child screen time at all.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Parents will use a coaching app themselves rather than hand the device to the child.
- **Validation test:** Concierge prompts sent by SMS for 2 weeks to 30 families. Measure prompt use (≥4 days/week) and parent-reported talk.

#### 2. Tap & Wonder (1–3)
- **Problem:** Toddler apps are loud, busy and drag-based. 3-year-olds succeed only ~73% of the time on basic touch tasks.
- **Experience:** One tap anywhere turns a scene into a gentle discovery (a real duck quacks softly, and the word "duck" is spoken). One concept per screen. The session winds down with a sleepy scene.
- **AI role:** Minimal. It picks the next scenes by variety and the parent's topic choices. **No** engagement optimization.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Calm by default. Targets ≥2.5 cm with whole-screen hit zones. No drag, pinch or double-tap. Captions of spoken words for Deaf parents co-playing.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Calm, finite play holds the toddler's attention long enough to have value, *and* parents see it as worth paying for.
- **Validation test:** In-home paper/Figma prototype sessions (n=12 dyads). Measure child engagement and the parent's perceived value.

#### 3. Story Lantern (2–7)
- **Problem:** Dialogic reading (adult asks questions and expands the child's words) strongly supports vocabulary, but parents don't know how to do it.
- **Experience:** A read-aloud library plus personalized stories (child as hero, family photos, their words). "Pause and ask" prompts for the adult. Screen-free **audio lantern mode** at bedtime.
- **AI role:** Drafts personalized stories from **human-authored templates**. Every story passes editorial safety filters. Suggests dialogic prompts matched to age.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Read-to-me with word highlighting. Adjustable narration speed. Dim, warm night theme. No sudden sounds.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Personalized AI stories add value over a curated library, and parents trust them.
- **Validation test:** Wizard-of-Oz, with a human writer producing "AI" stories for 20 families. Measure repeat listening and parent trust ratings.

#### 4. Sound Garden (4–7)
- **Problem:** Parents want their child reading but can't judge quality. Many apps reward tapping rather than decoding.
- **Experience:** A garden grows as the child masters sounds, blending and decodable books. The AI read-along listens as the child reads aloud and gives gentle help ("Let's sound it out together: /m/ /a/ /p/").
- **AI role:** Speech recognition **tuned for child speech**, with a tap fallback. Adaptive practice. **Early-risk signals** (for example, persistent difficulty with phonemic awareness) are shared carefully with parents, with guidance, and **no diagnosis**.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Dyslexia-friendly type. Reading-level dial. Tap alternative to speaking for children with speech delay. Calm celebrations.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Child ASR accuracy is good enough, or failures are handled well enough, that children don't get frustrated.
- **Validation test:** A read-along session with a human "listener" (Wizard-of-Oz), plus a benchmark of ASR word error rate on consented child speech samples, split by speaker group.

#### 5. Number Nest (3–7)
- **Problem:** Early math apps are timed drills, and number sense needs concrete manipulation.
- **Experience:** Digital Montessori-style materials (bead bars, ten-frames, balance scales) that **self-correct**: the scale tips when amounts don't match. Open-ended "make 5 in different ways".
- **AI role:** Chooses the next challenge from mastery. Offers hints in words and pictures.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Tap-to-select then tap-to-place (WCAG 2.5.7). Large targets. No timers.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Self-correcting play without scores motivates children aged 4–7.
- **Validation test:** Paper-prototype manipulative tasks in a pre-K class (n=16). Observe persistence without rewards.

#### 6. Calm Cubs (3–7)
- **Problem:** Transitions and big feelings cause daily conflict. Parents want tools that aren't just distraction.
- **Experience:** Feelings vocabulary with real faces and illustrated cubs. Breathing buddies with haptics. **Picture routines** with transition warnings (the Now/Next/Done strip). Co-regulation scripts for adults.
- **AI role:** Helps the parent build routines from a short description ("bedtime at 7:30, bath then 2 books"), with human-reviewed templates. **No emotion recognition.**
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Calm default. Visual, audio and haptic cues. Printable routine cards for screen-free use.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Parents will use the app in the moment of a meltdown, not only in calm moments.
- **Validation test:** A 1-week diary study with printable routines vs. the app prototype (n=20 families).

#### 7. Two Words (3–7)
- **Problem:** Bilingual families want to keep the home language alive, and most kids' apps are English-only.
- **Experience:** Paired vocabulary play (home language + English) and songs. Grandparents record words and stories in their own voices. Picture games for two generations.
- **AI role:** Suggests word sets. Pronunciation models in both languages. Grandparent recordings stay private to the family.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Voice-first for adults who read little. Large targets for grandparents. Captions.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Families will pay for heritage language, as opposed to EFL (where willingness to pay is proven in LatAm and Asia).
- **Validation test:** Landing-page smoke tests in the US (Spanish heritage) and Mexico and Brazil (EFL). Compare waitlist and price-point clicks.

## 5. Product principles (Lanternling-specific, on top of Lumen)
1. **The adult is part of the product.** Every experience has a co-play path.
2. **It stops.** Every session has a designed ending. We measure success by *good* sessions, not minutes.
3. **Real-world first.** Every screen activity points to an off-screen follow-up.
4. **Calm is the brand.** Sensory Dial on Calm by default for ages 1–3. No confetti, lives or timers.
5. **Science-backed, stated plainly.** Structured literacy, dialogic reading, Montessori manipulatives. We claim only the evidence tier we have reached.

## 6. Business model and go-to-market
- **Pricing (to validate):**
  - **Free forever:** Tap & Wonder basics, Babble Buddy prompts, 1 Story Lantern book/day.
  - **Family plan** ≈$9.99/mo or $69/yr for up to 3 children. Summer pause. Price shown before trial. One-tap cancel.
- **B2B2C:** Pre-K / Head Start / state pre-K programs, public libraries (card-holder access), pediatric practices ("Reach Out and Read"-style prescribing), EFL schools abroad.
- **Channels:** Parent communities, pediatric and SLP partners, science-of-reading creators, library partnerships, grandparent gifting.
- **Moat:**
  - child-speech models trained only on consented data (hard under COPPA 2025, and therefore defensible)
  - curriculum IP
  - a calm, trusted brand
  - kidSAFE/COPPA safe-harbor certification
  - pre-K relationships

## 7. Ethics, safety and compliance
- COPPA 2025: verifiable parental consent, separate consent for any third-party sharing or model training, written retention schedule.
- Child voice is processed on-device where feasible. No voice data is kept by default.
- No ads, no third-party trackers (Apple Kids category), no companion persona.
- Early reading-risk signals come with careful wording, resources and "talk to your pediatrician or teacher" guidance. **No diagnosis.**

## 8. Key risks and mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Free incumbents (Khan Kids, PBS KIDS) | Differentiate on co-play, calm, speech-aware reading and a toddler focus. A strong free tier |
| Child ASR errors frustrate children | Tap fallback everywhere. Human-in-the-loop evaluation. Group-level WER targets before launch |
| Summer churn / seasonality | Pause option, annual plans, family and grandparent value, library channel |
| "Screen time" stigma | Parent-held apps (Babble Buddy) and audio mode. Measure off-screen activities |
| Hardware temptation (Yoto/Tonies-style) | No hardware until retention is proven. Audio mode first |

## 9. Discovery and validation focus
**Riskiest assumptions to test first:** see the [Discovery Plan §5.1](../discovery/discovery-validation-plan.md).
1. Parents value and pay for calm, co-play-first apps (desirability + viability).
2. Toddler-parent pairs actually co-play when prompted (behavior).
3. Child-speech read-along is accurate enough to help, not frustrate (feasibility).
4. Pre-K and library partners will distribute (channel).

**North-star metric (proposed):** *Weekly co-play moments per family*, meaning a completed session plus a co-play card or off-screen follow-up. Guardrails: sessions end by design, and the Sensory Comfort Rating is ≥4/5.

**Three-year ambition (not a commitment):**
- Year 1: MVP of 2–3 apps (likely Babble Buddy, Sound Garden, Story Lantern) with evidence from a pre-registered pilot.
- Year 2: all 7 apps, plus pre-K and library contracts.
- Year 3: an ESSA Tier 2 study of Sound Garden, and international EFL.
