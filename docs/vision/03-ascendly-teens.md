# Ascendly: Vision Document
### AI-native learning, not cheating. Proof-of-learning and future pathways for ages 13–19

| | |
|---|---|
| **Venture** | 3 of 5 · Teens |
| **Segment** | **13–19**; secondary users are teachers, parents and school counselors |
| **One-liner** | Study with AI in a way that makes you smarter, and prove it. |
| **Mission** | Help teens build real understanding, confidence and direction in a world where AI can answer anything. |
| **Vision (5 yrs)** | The study platform teens choose *and* teachers trust. Its "proof-of-learning" record becomes part of how schools assess learning in the AI era, and its pathways connect teens to college, careers and AI-era skills. |
| **Business model** | B2C: a free core plus Plus (≈$10–15/mo) and seasonal exam passes ($50–150). B2B school licences for the proof-of-learning and assessment layer ($5–15 per student per year). Sponsored career pathways later. |
| **Phase** | Discovery & Validation (no code) |

---

## 1. Why now
- **AI cheating is the dominant topic in teen learning.**
  - Teachers are "rethinking, scaling back or abandoning homework".
  - A UC Irvine study of 3.2M ALEKS problems found the odds of a correct answer fell 25% after ChatGPT.
  - 58% of faculty name the erosion of critical thinking as their top concern.
- **Answer engines have peaked as a business.** Chegg's revenue fell 39% in 2025. Gauth, Photomath and Brainly raise grades while retention falls.
- **Free study modes exist but are opt-in and easy to switch off** (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot). Critics call them "a new conversation filter", and engagement fades after about 3 weeks. **No one owns teacher-verified learning.**
- **Teens want:**
  - free tools that let them export their data (the Quizlet → Knowt migration)
  - focus help (Forest, Opal)
  - social study (Discord, "study with me")
  - no surveillance ("it sends everything to your teacher… scary")
- **Phone bans in about 26–31 states** move teen study to home and after school, and "phone-free focus" is now a selling point.

### App cards

#### 1. Study Coach
- **Problem:** Teens already use AI to get answers, and it undermines exam performance.
- **Experience:** Ask about anything. The coach asks what you've tried, gives hints, shows a worked example and then fades it. "Teach it back" confirms understanding. The coach can recognize when a teen is under deadline pressure and offer a "study plan" instead of an answer.
- **AI role:**
  - LLM with tutoring policies and curriculum alignment.
  - Verified math and science steps.
  - Citations for factual claims.
  - AI disclosure. No companion persona.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Full VoiceOver/TalkBack. Math spoken via MathML. Voice or typed input. Dyslexia typography. Dark mode.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Teens choose Socratic help over free answer engines when deadlines loom.
- **Validation test:** Diary study plus a Wizard-of-Oz coach at two schools (n=40). Measure return rate and pre/post quiz gains.

#### 2. Exam Ready
- **Problem:** Test prep is expensive or scattered, and flashcard tools have gone behind paywalls.
- **Experience:** A diagnostic, then a personal plan. Retrieval practice with spaced review. Timed mock exams are *optional*. A readiness score with confidence bands. Flashcards are free and exportable.
- **AI role:** Item generation constrained by human-reviewed item banks. Error analysis. A study plan.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Extended-time settings matching accommodations (504/IEP). Screen-reader-friendly items. No required timers.
- **Riskiest assumption:** An aligned item bank of sufficient quality can be built affordably.
- **Validation test:** A 100-item pilot bank for one AP subject. Measure reviewer agreement and student-perceived quality.

#### 3. Explain It Back
- **Problem:** Teachers can't tell what a student actually understands. AI detectors are unreliable.
- **Experience:** A short oral, written or drawn explanation ("Explain why the reaction is exothermic"). AI gives rubric feedback. The best explanations are saved to the portfolio. Teachers get a class heat map of misconceptions.
- **AI role:** Rubric-based evaluation with teacher override. It flags possible misconceptions. It does **not** decide grades on its own (human oversight, in line with the EU AI Act for high-risk use).
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Answer in any mode (voice, text, AAC, drawing). Extra time. Captions.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Teachers will adopt proof-of-learning as homework, and teens won't feel surveilled.
- **Validation test:** Co-design with 8 teachers. Paper-prototype pilot in 4 classes. Measure the teacher-reported value of evidence and teen comfort ratings.

#### 4. Draft Mentor
- **Problem:** AI ghostwriting undermines writing skill and trust.
- **Experience:** Brainstorm, outline and draft in the app. The mentor asks questions and gives feedback on structure and evidence. It never writes paragraphs for you. An **authorship timeline** shows the drafting process, and the teen shares it only if they choose.
- **AI role:** Feedback and questioning within rubric limits. Generated text is clearly labeled and optional to include, and teachers can set that policy.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Dictation, text-to-speech, dyslexia-friendly editor, and a focus mode.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Teens will write inside our tool rather than in Google Docs.
- **Validation test:** A Google Docs add-on concept vs. a stand-alone app. Measure preference in interviews (n=30 teens, 10 teachers).

#### 5. Study Squad
- **Problem:** Studying alone with a phone in hand fails, especially for ADHD teens.
- **Experience:** Friends-only rooms with shared focus timers (body doubling). Video off by default. Emoji check-ins. Phone-free "lock-in" mode. Group goals.
- **AI role:** Suggests session plans. Moderation. No DMs with strangers.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Sound-optional. Visual timers. Low-stimulation room themes.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Teens will use a new social space rather than Discord.
- **Validation test:** A 2-week Discord pilot with a "Squad bot" (no build). Measure sessions per week and focus minutes.

#### 6. Pathfinder
- **Problem:** Career guidance is generic, and AI is changing entry-level jobs.
- **Experience:** 20-minute job simulations (nurse triage, electrician troubleshooting, UX designer brief). Reflection. A skills portfolio. Matching to dual enrollment, CTE and scholarships.
- **AI role:** Runs simulations as scenario role-play (clearly a simulation, not a companion). Skill mapping.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Accessible simulations. Alternatives for motor and sensory needs.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Schools, colleges or employers will pay (sponsored pathways).
- **Validation test:** Counselor interviews (n=15) plus a sponsor letter-of-intent test with 5 employers or community colleges.

#### 7. Life Ready
- **Problem:** Teens lack practical money, AI, media and civic skills, and scams target them.
- **Experience:** Short scenario challenges ("Your first paycheck", "Is this AI claim legit?"). Friend challenges. Real-world tasks.
- **AI role:** Scenario variation and feedback.
- **Accessibility & sensory:** Plain language. Captions. Screen reader.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Teens will engage without a school requirement.
- **Validation test:** A TikTok/Shorts content test for organic pull, plus school elective interest.

## 5. Product principles (Ascendly-specific)
1. **Learning mode is the default, not an opt-in.**
2. **Process over product.** We make thinking visible so honesty is easy.
3. **The teen owns the record.** They choose what to share. Summaries, not surveillance.
4. **Free core, export always.** Trust earned the Knowt way.
5. **Accessible from day one.** A blind or dyslexic teen gets the full product.
6. **Respect teens.** Age-respectful design, no childish gamification, no manipulative streaks.

## 6. Business model and go-to-market
- **B2C:** free core. Plus (≈$12/mo or $79/yr) unlocks unlimited coaching, all exams and Pathfinder. Seasonal exam passes. Honest billing.
- **B2B:** proof-of-learning licences for schools ($5–15 per student per year). Teacher freemium seeds adoption.
- **Channels:**
  - teen-native short-form content (organic, no dark patterns)
  - teacher communities
  - school counselors
  - CTE and dual-enrollment partners
  - state AI-literacy initiatives
- **Moat:**
  - teachers relying on the integrity and assessment tooling
  - exam-aligned item banks
  - score-gain data
  - learner-owned portfolios that carry into college and careers

## 7. Ethics, safety and compliance
- Ages 13–17: AADC and state design codes, KOSA-ready (no compulsive-use features), privacy-protective defaults, parental controls **with teen notice**.
- No companion or romance persona (FTC 6(b), CA SB 243). Distress escalation based on what the teen says, with crisis resources.
- FERPA/SOPIPA for school deployments. Signed DPAs. No ad targeting.
- EU: rubric-based evaluation is designed for high-risk obligations (human oversight, logging) ahead of Dec 2027.

## 8. Risks and mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Commoditization by free lab study modes | Own exam depth, teacher-verified learning, portfolios, social focus and accessibility |
| Teens perceive surveillance | Teen-controlled sharing. Only summaries go to teachers. Co-design with teens |
| Seasonal churn | Exam passes, a school channel and year-round Pathfinder |
| Content accuracy | Verified solvers, human-reviewed banks, citations |

## 9. Discovery and validation focus
- **Top assumptions:** teens choose learning mode under deadline pressure; teachers adopt proof-of-learning; Study Squad beats Discord for focus; sponsors pay for pathways.
- **North-star:** *Weekly verified understanding moments* (Explain It Back passes plus mastery checks). Guardrails: teen comfort and privacy score, and accessibility audit ≥22/24.
- **Three-year ambition:**
  - Year 1: Study Coach + Exam Ready (1–2 exams) + Explain It Back pilot in 10 schools.
  - Year 2: Draft Mentor, Study Squad.
  - Year 3: Pathfinder sponsors, and a published score-gain study.
