## 2. Problem & users
**Problem statement.**
- Many social-skills programs aim to make autistic children *appear* typical: more eye contact, suppressed stims, scripted small talk. Autistic self-advocates and a growing academic literature criticize this as training in **masking**. Camouflaging has been associated with poorer mental-health outcomes [V2, raw 04 sources: [Child Mind Institute](https://childmind.org/article/controversy-around-applied-behavior-analysis/), [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11219658/), [MDPI 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4698/15/3/72), [Disability & Society 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09687599.2025.2478049)]; specific camouflaging studies [M: e.g., Cassidy et al.].
- The **double empathy problem** holds that communication breakdowns between autistic and non-autistic people are mutual, not a one-sided deficit [M: Milton 2012]. Experimental work found information transfer between autistic pairs comparable to non-autistic pairs, with breakdowns in mixed pairs [M: Crompton et al. 2020].
- **Social narratives are an evidence-based practice** (NCAEP 2020) for social, communication and behavioral outcomes [V] ([NCAEP](https://ncaep.fpg.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/EBP-Report-2020.pdf)). The *method* is sound. The *goals and framing* are what we change.
- Parents still pay for "social skills", and schools write IEP goals around them. The risk is being affirming but unsellable (vision assumption W3).

**Evidence benchmark [M]:** Manualized group programs such as **PEERS** (UCLA) have RCT evidence for teen social-skills *knowledge* and some social outcomes. Social-skills group meta-analyses show modest effects, with weaker generalization. No app in this list has product-level RCT evidence for social outcomes [I].

**Voices of autistic self-advocates (summarized, not quoted):** Common critiques [V2, raw 04 sources; M for specifics] are:
1. Programs that teach eye contact, "quiet hands" or suppression of stims are harmful.
2. "Expected vs. unexpected" framing puts the whole burden on the autistic person.
3. Emotion-recognition drills with posed faces assume one "correct" reading.
4. Autistic people are rarely paid co-authors.
Our spec answers each one.

## 5. Core experience & key user flows
**Core loop:** choose a situation (or one from "coming up this week") → explore both sides → pick something to try → (optional) practise → real life → reflect → update the profile card or scripts.

**Flow 1: Onboarding (≤5 min)**
1. Calm dial; text-first by default for 12+; age band; look (neutral / teen / illustrated).
2. Language: "How do you like to talk about yourself?" with identity-first, person-first and "prefer not to say" options.
3. "What would help most?" Understanding people, saying what I need, group chats, a card for teachers.
4. The first item is a short scenario or the profile-card starter.

**Flow 2: Core session (Ethan, group project)**
1. Scenario: "My group changed the plan in the chat without asking me."
2. Ethan explores his side (frustrated, the plan was clear) and their side (maybe they thought quick replies meant agreement).
3. Options for him ("Ask: can we confirm changes before Friday?") and for them ("Check with everyone before changing").
4. He saves a script. Optionally, he practises with a labelled "Practice classmate" in 6 turns.
5. Reflection prompt later in the week (opt-in reminder).

**Flow 3: SLP / parent view**
1. Ms. Carter runs group mode with 5 students, and anonymous choices spark discussion.
2. She sees which scripts students saved, only if students share.
3. Parents get the adult guides and the teen's card only if the teen shares it.

**Flow 4: My Needs:** text/audio, reading level, Dial, role-play on/off (adult-controlled under 13), sharing controls.

**Flow 5: Billing:** Fair-billing charter; the profile card and scripts are free forever.

**Information architecture:** Explore · My scripts · My card · Practice · Reflect. Adult: Guides · Group mode · Goals.

**Session design:** 5–15 min; each scenario ends on "What I'd like to try" and a designed ending. No streaks.

## 6. Inclusive, accessible & sensory design spec
**Sensory Dial:** Calm (static illustrations, no sound) · Balanced (subtle transitions, optional narration) · Lively (animated panels, no flashing).

**Text-first:** All scenarios work as plain text with a read-aloud option. No voice input is required, and there are **no face or eye-contact tasks**.

**Input modes:** Tap, keyboard, voice (optional), AAC (Voice symbols can answer choices and build scripts), switch and eye gaze (choices are discrete buttons).

**Targets:** Platform standard or larger; no drag.

**Reading:** Grade 3–8 dial; BDA typography; glossary for idioms ("What does 'break a leg' mean?"). Idioms are explained, never used unexplained in instructions.

**Audio:** Optional narration by diverse voices, including autistic narrators; captions always on for video (V1).

**Age-respectful themes:** Neutral/teen photo and graphic styles for 12+; illustrated for 8–11; independent of reading level.

**Representation:** Characters include autistic, ADHD, AAC-using, stimming, disabled, LGBTQ+ (age-appropriate, reviewed) and culturally diverse people. The autistic character is not always the one who "gets it wrong".

## 7. AI specification & guardrails
**What AI does**
- **Role-play scene partner (S4):** An LLM plays a named *role* (e.g., "Practice teacher") in a bounded scenario authored by humans. A scenario card sets the goal, the character's perspective and the stop conditions. Maximum 10 turns. The UI permanently labels it "Practice character (AI)". A reflection screen closes the session.
- **Script tone variants (S2):** An LLM rewrites the learner's script into short, polite or formal versions. The learner picks or edits. The original is always kept.

**What AI does not do**
- No companion persona, friend talk or memory across sessions.
- No romantic or sexual role-play (refused at every age).
- No emotion recognition of the learner (no camera or voice analysis).
- No judgment of the learner as "socially correct".
- No persuasion toward masking. The model is instructed and evaluated to refuse "teach me to act normal / stop stimming / make more eye contact" and to reframe toward self-advocacy and mutual understanding.

**Safety**
- **Self-report distress:** If the learner types about bullying, self-harm or abuse, role-play stops, a supportive message appears with trusted-adult and crisis resources, and a caregiver or school alert is sent **only** under the disclosed safeguarding policy (school mode) or with the teen's consent (home mode, except imminent-risk rules, which are disclosed up front).
- **Content:** Filters for sexual content, hate and self-harm instructions.
- **Transparency:** A child-readable "what the practice character is" card is shown before first use.

**Evaluation plan**
- An affirming-stance eval set of 300 prompts, written with autistic adults, covering masking requests, stereotypes and "fix me" framings. **Ship threshold:** ≥98% compliant responses and 0 severe violations.
- Scenario fidelity: human rating ≥4/5.
- **ND board sign-off** on the model behavior spec. Monthly sampling of 2% of role-plays with consent.

**Cost [E]:** ≈$0.01–0.03 per role-play session.

- **COPPA 2025:** VPC for under-13s; role-play for 8–12 only with an adult present (V1).
- **Teens:** Parental controls with child notice (UK AADC standard [V2]). The teen decides what to share.
- **FERPA** for school group mode; **HIPAA** for practice licences.
- **EU AI Act:** Art. 5 no emotion recognition; Art. 50 transparency for AI characters [V2].
- **California SB 243 / FTC AI-companion scrutiny** [M]: our role-play is non-companion by design. Document it in a published AI-character policy.
- **FTC:** no social-skills efficacy claims without evidence.

## 10. Success metrics
- **North star:** Weekly self-advocacy actions reported.
- **Input metrics:** Scripts saved; profile cards created and shared; scenarios explored per week; reflections logged.
- **Guardrails:**
  - Learner "this felt respectful" ≥4.5/5
  - Autistic-adult reviewer approval 100% before release
  - Sensory Comfort ≥4/5
  - 0 severe AI violations
  - 0 billing complaints
- **Outcomes:** Self-advocacy self-efficacy (scale chosen with the advisory board [M]); teacher/parent understanding (the double-empathy half); learner well-being self-report. Do not measure "reduced autistic behaviors". **Evidence:** E3 → E2 mixed-methods pilot co-led with autistic researchers.
- **Retention [E]:** Use is episodic. 60-day active ≥35%.

## 12. Build handoff
**Epic A: Scenario explorer**
- *Given* any scenario, *then* it presents both perspectives and ≥2 valid options for each party, with no score shown.
- *Given* a scenario lacking ND-board approval metadata, *then* it cannot be published (CMS gate).

**Epic B: Scripts & card**
- *Given* a saved script, *when* the learner taps "Send to Voice", *then* it appears as a quick phrase in Wavelength Voice.
- *Given* a shared card link, *then* it expires by default after 30 days, and the teen can revoke it at any time.

**Epic C: Role-play**
- *Given* a role-play session, *then* the "Practice character (AI)" label is visible on every turn and the session ends by turn 10 with a reflection.
- *Given* the learner requests masking coaching, *then* the model responds with a reframe per the policy and logs a safety-eval event (content-free).

**Non-functional requirements:** WCAG 2.2 AA; text-first performance on low-end devices; iOS/Android/web; EN-US/EN-GB at launch.

**QA focus**
- **AT matrix:** VoiceOver/TalkBack full scenario flow, Switch Control, Eye Tracking, AAC-driven answers (Voice integration), Dynamic Type XXL, Reduce Motion, Full Keyboard Access.
- **Sensory A/B.**
- **AI:** affirming-stance eval, safety pathways, jailbreaks toward companion or romance.
- **COPPA/teen privacy:** sharing controls.
- **Billing:** free-core guarantee.

**Platform dependencies:** Lumen, Circle (card sharing), Voice (scripts to quick phrases), AI orchestration (policy engine, eval harness), privacy stack.

## 13. Risks & open questions
