# Top 30 Learning / Education Apps on Google Play (Global, US notes) — Sept 2026

**Prepared:** 2026-09-29 · **Scope:** Google Play Education category plus learning apps listed in other categories (Kids/Family, Games-Educational, Health & Fitness, Productivity). Global view, with US notes where relevant.

## Method and confidence

- **Ranking rule:** (1) current Google Play lifetime install band, then (2) 2025 annual downloads (Appfigures/AppMagic via Business of Apps, all platforms) where published, then (3) lifetime-download estimates from trackers and review volume.
- **Data access:** Live Play Store, AppBrain, Appfigures and Business of Apps pages could not be fetched directly from this environment (egress blocked). Figures come from search-engine snippets of those pages, captured Sept 2026, plus analyst knowledge.
- **Confidence tags:** **(V)** = seen in a Sept-2026 search snippet of the Play listing or a tracker. **(E)** = analyst estimate or recalled pricing and features. Check these before quoting externally. Prices are US list prices and change often.
- **General-purpose AI assistants** (ChatGPT, Gemini, Google Lens) are big "tutors" by usage but are not learning apps. They are in a separate context table so they don't distort the ranking.
- **Removed from the 2023–24-era lists:** BYJU'S (100M+, delisted from Play in May 2025 over unpaid AWS bills), Microsoft Math Solver (retired 7 Jul 2025) and Socratic by Google (folded into Google Lens, app removed from about Oct 2024).

### Headline market numbers (Business of Apps / Appfigures, 2025)
- More than 1B education-app downloads in 2025, about 800M users, $6.4B revenue (+6.7% YoY), and North America is 42% of spend.
- Top 10 by 2025 downloads (all platforms): Duolingo 178M · Seekho 92M · Gauth 51M · Learna 32M · Google Classroom 30M · Quizlet 26M · Guru 23M · Minecraft Education 23M · Impulse 22M · Photomath 19M. (Business of Apps also cites Duolingo at 172M.)
- Language-learning apps: 327M downloads in 2025. Online-course apps: only about 29M.

## Analysis — what wins, where the gaps are

**1. The winners are habit machines, not courseware.** The top of the chart (Duolingo, Seekho, Cake, Kahoot!, Quizlet) share the same loop: sessions under 5 minutes, visible streaks and XP, social comparison (leagues, live leaderboards) and push notifications. Duolingo's 500M+ installs and 178M downloads in 2025 are almost twice the next app, and its moat is the streak rather than its pedagogy. Seekho shows the loop moving to vertical short video: 92M downloads in 2025 and 28–29 minutes a day, in Hindi. Long-form course apps (Coursera, Udemy) are shrinking on mobile, with the whole online-course subsector at only ~29M downloads in 2025.

**2. AI homework solvers are the fastest-growing category.** Gauth (ByteDance, 100M+, 51M in 2025) grew from ~288K weekly downloads in late 2024 to become the #3 education app worldwide. Question.AI, Brainly's AI pivot and Photomath (now Google's) complete a "snap-and-solve" cluster aimed at 13–19s. Generalist AI is closing in from above: ChatGPT Study Mode (Jul 2025) and Gemini Guided Learning (Aug 2025, free year for students) are free, Socratic-style modes. Meanwhile Google retired Socratic, and Microsoft retired Math Solver. **AI language tutors** are a second wave: Learna reached 32M downloads in 2025, and Praktika, Speak, Loora, Stimuler and ELSA each have 10M+ installs, selling speaking practice without the fear of a human listener. What these apps sell is either an answer (homework) or confidence (speaking). Few of them sell proof of learning.

**3. Monetisation is converging on paywall-first subscriptions, and reviews push back.** Almost every top-30 app has a paywall complaint ("steps moved behind Plus", "hearts", weekly trial auto-renew). Quizlet, Photomath and Duolingo all show paywall creep in their reviews. The free and trusted apps (Khan Academy, Khan Kids, Read Along, PBS KIDS) are rated highly but under-distributed, which leaves room for "fair freemium".

**4. Gaps by age segment**
- **1–3:** Almost nothing purpose-built in the top 30. Lingokids and Khan Kids reach down to 2–3, but toddler content is mostly YouTube. There's a gap for parent-mediated, low-screen, speech and vocabulary co-play.
- **4–7:** Well served but split between subscription-heavy apps (Lingokids, ABCmouse) and free apps (Khan Kids, PBS KIDS, Read Along). Few apps use AI adaptively for early literacy beyond Read Along.
- **8–12:** Under-served. It is too old for preschool apps and too young for Gauth and ChatGPT, whose ToS restrict under-13s. Minecraft Education and Kahoot! are school-gated. A COPPA-safe AI tutor for this age is a clear gap.
- **13–19:** Over-served with answer engines and under-served with tools that build real understanding and that teachers trust.
- **20–34 / 35–59:** Language and brain training are crowded. Workplace upskilling on mobile (Seekho-style) is proven in India but has no US/EU equivalent at scale.
- **60+:** No app targets this group, yet Babbel, Elevate and Impulse draw heavily on older adults. There's room for cognitive health plus digital-literacy learning with large type and calm UI.

**5. Accessibility and sensory gaps.** Most top apps rely on timers (Kahoot!, Impulse, Quizlet Match), drag-and-drop tiles, camera-first input or voice-first scoring. Each of these excludes some learners: blind users, users with motor impairments, dyslexic users (no dyslexia fonts in the major apps) or speech-impaired users (ELSA, Praktika, Read Along). Math output from solvers is rarely screen-reader friendly. High-stimulus reward animations are standard, and no top-30 app offers a real **low-sensory or reduced-motion mode** for neurodivergent learners. Khan Kids and Babbel come closest by design, not through settings.

**6. White-space opportunities**
1. **"Show your thinking" AI tutor for 8–15.** Socratic by default, with teacher and parent visibility and no answer copying. It would target the trust gap between Gauth and Khanmigo.
2. **Neuro-inclusive learning layer.** Dyslexia-friendly type, no timers, reduced motion and sound, predictable UI and an optional "calm streak" (weekly goals, no loss aversion). This could be a product or a white-label SDK.
3. **Toddler/parent co-play (1–3)** built on speech-development milestones, with minimal screen time.
4. **Vernacular micro-skills video plus AI practice** for US/LatAm adult upskilling (Seekho's model).
5. **60+ learning and brain-health** with large type, voice interaction and clear pricing (no weekly trial traps).
6. **Accessible STEM solver** that outputs MathML/speech-friendly steps and supports non-camera input.
