# Apple App Store: Top 30 Learning / Education Apps (US-primary, global notes)

*Prepared 2026-09-29 for the EdTech startup studio. Market-research raw file #02.*

---

## 0. Method, data quality and caveats (read first)

- **Live data access was partial.** WebSearch worked. Direct page fetches (apps.apple.com, Apple RSS charts, Sensor Tower, Appfigures, AppBrain, Business of Apps, mwm.ai) were **blocked by the network egress proxy**. Every figure below comes from search-result snippets of those sources, published company figures (e.g., Duolingo's SEC filings), or analyst knowledge. **Treat all download numbers as estimates.**
- **The ranking basis is estimated US App Store (iPhone + iPad) downloads over the trailing 12 months (about Oct 2025 to Sep 2026).** It combines these anchors:
  - **Appfigures, 2025 full year (global, iOS + Google Play, Education):** Duolingo 178M · Seekho 92M (India-only, mostly Android) · Gauth 51M · Learna 32M · Google Classroom 30M · Quizlet 26M · Guru 23M · Minecraft Education 23M · Impulse 22M · Photomath 19M.
  - **Sensor Tower, US iOS weekly data for kids' "educational games", Q1–Q2 2025:** Lingokids 48–72K/week · ABCmouse 38–57K/week · Kiddopia 18–23K/week · Keiki 21–22K/week · Speech Blubs ~14K/week. That works out to roughly 3M, 2.5M, 1M, 1.1M and 0.6M per year.
  - **Sensor Tower, Gauth US iOS overview:** about 900K downloads in the latest month. Gauth also held **#1 in US Education downloads for 6 consecutive days in Q3 2025** and ranked #2 in Education (behind Duolingo) in April 2024.
  - **Recent US Top Free Education chart (2026 snippets):** #1 Duolingo, #2 Learna, #3 Gauth. **Headway was #1 in US and UK Education in January 2025.**
- **The download column shows ranges** marked **[E]** (estimate). Ratings marked **[R]** are App Store figures seen in search results from 2025–26; a value marked **[E]** is analyst knowledge and needs checking.
- **General-purpose AI assistants** (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) and **Apple's own apps** are covered in a separate benchmark table (section 3). They would dominate a pure download ranking but are not learning-specific.
- **Design benchmarks that fall outside the top 30 by volume** (Pok Pok, Endless Alphabet/Originator, Tinybop, HOMER, MasterClass, Blinkist, Imprint, Mimo, Sololearn, Yousician, Lumosity) are in section 4.
- **About the accessibility notes.** Apple's **Accessibility Nutrition Labels** (announced May 2025, voluntary at first, mandatory later) let a developer declare VoiceOver, Voice Control, Larger Text, Dark Interface, Differentiate Without Color, Sufficient Contrast, Reduced Motion, Captions and Audio Descriptions. The notes below come from public reviews and forums (AppleVis, Family Gaming Database, Common Sense) and analyst inspection. They are **not verified label declarations**, so re-check each product page before relying on one.

**Age segments:** 1–3 · 4–7 · 8–12 · 13–19 · 20–34 · 35–59 · 60+

## 5. Analysis (≈650 words)

**What wins on iOS vs. Android.** Android wins on volume: Education is Google Play's largest category by installs, and the global top 10 includes Android-heavy, India-centric apps (Seekho 92M, Guru 23M) that barely appear on the US App Store. iOS wins on value. Education is only about the 4th-largest iOS category, yet it over-indexes in wealthy markets, and iOS earns most of the category's ≈$6.4B in 2025 consumer spend. The US iOS chart therefore favors apps that can charge: Duolingo (12.7M paid subscribers), AI tutors with hard paywalls (Learna, Speak, Praktika), and premium kids subscriptions (Lingokids, ABCmouse, Kiddopia). AI homework solvers (Gauth, Photomath, Question.AI) show up on both platforms. On iOS they gain from peaks when US school starts: Gauth held #1 in US Education for 6 straight days in Q3 2025.

**Premium willingness is real but stretched.** US iOS users pay $80–$170/yr for Duolingo Max, Simply Piano and Brilliant, and $45–$100/yr for kids apps. The same apps' reviews, though, are dominated by **billing anger**: trial-to-annual surprises, weekly pricing, hard cancellation (the FTC settlement against ABCmouse, Gauth's billing loop), and features moved behind paywalls (Quizlet's Learn and Test, Duolingo's Energy). App Store star ratings (4.3–4.9) hide this; Trustpilot scores (1.4–3.8) expose it. Pricing that is honest and transparent (Khan Academy Kids is free; Pok Pok offers a lifetime option) is itself a way to stand out.

**Apple's privacy and Kids-category rules shape the kids market.** Guideline 1.3 bans third-party ads and analytics that send personal or device data in Kids-category apps, and requires parental gates. The 2025 Declared Age Range API and the redesigned child-account setup push age assurance onto the platform. Together these rules make ad-funded kids apps nearly impossible on iOS. The result is two viable models: **subscription** (Lingokids, ABCmouse, Piknik) or **mission-funded free** (PBS KIDS, Khan Academy Kids). Privacy is also a way to win trust: ByteDance-owned Gauth and opaquely owned solvers carry reputational risk with parents and districts.

**Design excellence patterns.** Apple's editors keep rewarding the same things. **Calm, non-coercive design**: Pok Pok (2021 ADA) with no scores and soft sound; Tiimo (2025 App of the Year) with soothing timelines. **Delight without fail states**: Endless Alphabet, Tinybop, Sago Mini. **Novel native interaction**: CapWords (2025 ADA) turns camera captures into vocabulary. **Inclusivity**: Speechify (2025 ADA), Guitar Wiz (2026 ADA), and Sago Mini Jinja's Garden (2026 finalist). The download leaders run on the opposite playbook: streaks, leagues, loud celebrations, and paywall friction. That gap is open for a well-designed product that also grows well.

**Accessibility and sensory gaps.**
1. **Screen readers.** Duolingo, the category leader, is repeatedly reported as broken or "an afterthought" with VoiceOver. Most kids apps are image-only with no VoiceOver model at all. Camera-first AI solvers exclude low-vision students.
2. **Text scaling.** Dynamic Type support is rare in kids and game-like apps; Khan Academy Kids' library text reportedly cannot be enlarged.
3. **Sensory load.** Most top kids apps (ABCmouse, Kiddopia, Lingokids, Prodigy, Toca) are high-stimulation, and few honor Reduce Motion or offer quiet modes.
4. **Speech-first AI tutors** (Speak, Learna, Praktika) exclude users with speech disabilities unless they offer a text alternative.
5. **Accessibility Nutrition Labels** (voluntary since 2025, mandatory later) will make these gaps visible and searchable ("VoiceOver language app"). Early adopters in education will gain search visibility.

**White-space opportunities by age segment.**
- **1–3:** almost nobody besides Pok Pok and Sago Mini makes calm, co-play-first, Reduced-Motion-aware toddler apps. Build around parent-child co-use and a sensory-intensity dial.
- **4–7:** early literacy for dyslexic and neurodivergent children (phonics with Read-to-Me, OpenDyslexic-style type, low-stim mode). Bilingual Spanish/English at PBS quality but with depth.
- **8–12:** a gap between Prodigy's "pay-to-win" pressure and school LMS apps. A Socratic AI tutor that never hands over answers, is Kids-category compliant, and gives parents transparency has no clear leader, since Khanmigo is web-first.
- **13–19:** Gauth and Photomath own "answers". The white space is **integrity-safe AI study** (Study Mode-like, but mobile-native, curriculum-aligned and visible to teachers) with full VoiceOver and Dynamic Type.
- **20–34 / 35–59:** AI speaking tutors are crowded. Opportunities lie in vocational and career micro-credentials on mobile, and in accessible speech alternatives.
- **60+:** clearly underserved. Brain-training apps (Elevate, Lumosity, Impulse) rely on timed games that are poor for aging eyes and motor control. Large-type, voice-guided, unhurried learning (languages, tech skills, music, genealogy and history) is open, and this group pays well on iOS.
